# ReelBolt Individual Contributor License Agreement

Version 1.0. This agreement is modeled on the Apache Software Foundation's Individual Contributor License Agreement, with an added section (3) that lets the project owner offer the project under more than one licence.

## In plain language

This is not legal advice, and the numbered text below is what counts.

- **You keep your copyright.** You are not handing your code over.
- **You give the project owner a broad licence to your contribution**, including the right to relicense it. ReelBolt is released under the GNU Affero General Public License v3 (AGPL-3.0) and is also offered to some customers under a separate commercial licence. For that to be possible, the project owner needs permission to use every contribution under both.
- **The open-source release stays open.** Your contribution will always be available to the public under the AGPL-3.0 or a licence that is at least as open (see section 3).
- **You promise it is yours to give.** The code is your own work, or you have the right to contribute it, and you tell us about anything that is not yours.
- **You sign once**, by posting one sentence on your first pull request. That covers every contribution you make later.
- **Contributing for an employer?** Read the corporate note at the end first.

## How to sign

Open a pull request. A bot checks whether you have signed. If you have not, it posts a comment asking you to reply with exactly this sentence:

> I have read the CLA Document and I hereby sign the CLA

The bot records your GitHub username, user ID, the pull request and the date on the `cla-signatures` branch of the repository. The record is public, because everything on that branch is public. You can ask for your entry to be removed together with your Contributions, but removal does not undo licences already granted for code that has been released.

## Corporate contributions

If you contribute as part of your work, or your employer may claim rights to what you write, **ask your employer to sign the corporate agreement before you contribute.** It covers a named list of employees who may contribute for the company, and it is the safe path when an employer's policy requires approval for open-source work. The corporate agreement is available on request: write to **legal@reelbolt.ai** with the company name and the GitHub usernames to cover. Until the project owner confirms a corporate agreement for your employer, individual signatures from employees do not extend to their employer's rights.

Not sure whether your employer has rights? Ask before you contribute. Please do not submit code from your employer's systems, or based on non-public employer material, with an individual signature.
