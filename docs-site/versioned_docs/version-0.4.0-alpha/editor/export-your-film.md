# Export your film

When your edit is ready, open **Deliver** (the editor's **More > Export** menu opens it). The editor's **Early preview** label stays on this page like everywhere else in the editor.

On a computer or a tablet the page has two sides. On the left you choose what to render: a **preset**, its options, and a short statement of what will be made and what it costs, with the **Render** button under it. On the right is everything that comes out: the latest film, the render queue, review links, export for other apps and the hand-off package.

## Your film comes first

The **Latest film** section shows your latest finished film: a player, the file name, how long it is, how big the file is and how long ago it was made. Under the player:

- **Download** saves the film to your computer under its own name.
- **Open in project files** takes you to your project's Files page with the film selected.
- **Copy link** copies the address of the film in your project. People who have access to the project can open it. It is not a public link.
- **Delete** asks first, then removes the film from your project files. Your timeline is not changed. If you can only look at the timeline, Delete is not offered.

Below the buttons, a line tells you whether the film has your latest edits: "Last render: your latest edit", or "Last render: before your latest edits (3 changes since)".

## Choose a preset and press Render {#choose-a-format-and-press-render}

**Presets** is the list at the top of the left side. Each row is one kind of film and says what it makes: its size, its shape and what it is for. One preset is always chosen, and the list is never folded away.

- **Full HD landscape**, **4K landscape**, **Vertical for phones**, **Square** and **Portrait 4:5** are fixed shapes. If the shape does not match your timeline, you are told the picture will be cropped or get bars.
- **Master** renders at your timeline's own size at the highest quality, to keep or to finish somewhere else.
- **Audio only** makes a sound file with no picture.

The list tells you what your plan does with each preset before you click anything. A preset your plan cannot render is marked **Not in your plan** and cannot be chosen; a line under the list says why and links to the plans. A preset that is larger than your plan allows still renders: its row says the size it will come out at.

**Options** holds what refines the preset you chose:

- **Quality** is Draft (fastest, smallest), Good, or Best (cleanest, slower, bigger file).
- **Only the marked in/out range** renders just the part between your in and out marks. Until both marks are set, the switch tells you which keys set them.
- **File name** is optional. Leave it blank to name the film after the timeline.
- **Captions**, when your timeline has captions, chooses between burning them into the picture, separate files only, or both.
- **Size and quality** and **Advanced** fold away the file size, loudness normalising and the codec and speed choices for people who need them.

The **Render** button says what it will make, for example "Render vertical film". It saves your latest edits first, so the film always contains what is on screen. When Render is off, a line above it says why and what to do: the timeline is empty, your plan does not include this size, the file name has a character that cannot be used, you need more credits, or your latest edits could not be saved ("Save first:" with the reason, and a way to try saving again).

## Save your own presets

Under the list, **My presets** keeps settings you use often.

1. Set the page up the way you want: the preset, the quality, the file size and loudness settings.
2. Press **Save these settings as a preset** and give it a name.
3. Next time, press **Apply** on that preset. A preset whose settings are on the page right now says **In use**.

**Rename** and **Delete** are on each preset; Delete asks first. A preset holds the format, quality, size, encoder and loudness settings. The range, the file name and the captions choice belong to each render, so they are not saved.

Your presets are kept in this browser, on this device only. They do not follow you to another computer, and clearing the browser's site data removes them. If your browser does not let the page keep anything (private browsing, for example), the section says so and the rest of the page works as usual.

## Set the file size

Under **Size and quality**, **How to set the file size** has three choices:

- **Steady look** keeps the picture looking the same. The file size depends on your video.
- **Look first, with a size limit** keeps the look and the file close to or under the video bitrate you set. It is a target, so the file can run slightly over.
- **Aim for a size** aims for the video bitrate you set, so the file ends up close to the size shown under the setting.

Set **Video bitrate (Mbps)** from 0.2 to 100 Mbps and **Audio bitrate (kbps)** from 64 to 320. The size shown is always approximate ("About"), because the real file depends on your video. An audio-only film has no video bitrate.

**Suggested for** fills the settings for a destination. These are suggested starting points, not a promise that a site accepts the file. YouTube's 8 Mbps is its published recommendation for 1080p; Instagram Reels and TikTok publish no bitrate, so those values are our own suggestions.

| Destination | Format | File size setting | Video | Audio |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| YouTube | Full HD landscape | Aim for a size | 8 Mbps | 320 kbps |
| Instagram Reels | Vertical for phones | Look first, with a size limit | 5 Mbps | 128 kbps |
| TikTok | Vertical for phones | Look first, with a size limit | 6 Mbps | 128 kbps |
| Small for email or chat | Your current format | Aim for a size | 2.5 Mbps | 128 kbps |

The bitrate does not change the price: a render costs the same at 2 Mbps and at 100 Mbps.

## What it costs

**Before you render** is one statement that stays in view above the Render button. It says:

- **Format**: the preset and the size of the picture, with the size your plan will export it at when that is smaller.
- **Range**: the whole timeline or your marked range, with its start and end as timecode.
- **Captions**: where they go, when your timeline has captions.
- **Cost**: about how many credits the render uses and about how long it takes, the range it usually falls in, and how many credits you have.

The figures come from the same estimate the render itself uses, for exactly the settings on the page. When you change a setting, the statement says it is working the cost out and then shows the new figures; it never shows the old cost next to new settings.

- If you do not have enough credits, the statement says how many more you need and offers **Add credits**, and Render stays off.
- If the cost cannot be worked out right now, it says so and offers **Try again**. Your credits are still checked before a render starts.
- If credits are not part of your plan, there is no cost line: the statement says **Ready to render**.

## Export only one clip

Select a clip, open Deliver and press **Export only "clip name"**. One press sets the range to that clip and starts the render. While it works, the panel says "Rendering "clip name"". Your in and out marks stay set to the clip afterwards.

## Export for other apps

The **Export for other apps** section saves your cut as a file another program can read. It uses no credits.

1. Choose the **File type**.
2. Read what that file carries and what it leaves out. The two lists come from the same place that writes the file, so they describe the download exactly. A line under them says whether the file points at your media.
3. Press **Download** for that type.

The other file types are listed under the download for people who already know which one they want. The two caption file types are offered only when your timeline has captions. The files contain what is already saved.

## For another editor

The **Hand-off package** section prepares one package for a professional editor: the cut, your video files under their real names, a list of what is inside and a short note on how to open it.

1. Choose the editor: Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve, Final Cut Pro, another editor, or **Cuts only (EDL)**. Premiere Pro and DaVinci Resolve can take either of two file types; the card picks the first.
2. Choose **Include the video files** or **List files only**. The card shows what the package holds, for example "12 files, 1.4 GB". When the files together are too big for one package, the first option is switched off, the card says why, and **List files only** is chosen for you. Copy the originals yourself then.
3. For **Cuts only (EDL)** with more than one video track, pick which video track to write.
4. Press **Download the package**. The file is saved by your browser. If something stops it (for example two packages are already being prepared), the card says so in words.

Hand-off packages carry an **Early preview** label. We have not yet opened one in Premiere Pro, DaVinci Resolve or Final Cut Pro, so treat them as a first version.

**How to open it** gives three lines per editor. We have not opened these packages in every editor, so the lines say "Should open in ..." and ask you to tell us if it does not.

On a phone the card only says that this is a desktop job; the export links above still work.

## Importing a timeline from another editor

On your timelines page, **Import** reads an OpenTimelineIO file. When it is done, a sheet says what happened: the files it matched (and how: same file as the export, matched by name, matched by name and length) and the clips whose media is **offline**, each with the reason:

- "No file with this name in the project"
- "Two files share this name; pick one"
- "The file's length differs from the clip"

Each offline clip is a gap of its own length with a red marker. **Relink** puts a project file there; for two files with the same name **Pick a file** lists them with their length and size. Relinking is one step you can undo. The switch **Import fails if anything is missing** (off by default) refuses the whole import instead. Relinking a whole folder at once is not available yet.

## The render list

The **Render queue** section, under the latest film, lists every render of this timeline, newest first: Waiting for a free slot, Running (with progress and a **Stop** button), Done, Failed or Cancelled. Each row says its format and, once it is finished, how long the film is, how big the file is and when it was made.

- A finished row has **Download**. If the render made caption files, they are listed on the row too.
- A render that did not finish says why in plain words and offers **Try again** (with the settings shown now) and **Remove**.
- **Render again** puts the settings a render was started with back on the page: the preset, the quality and file size settings, the captions choice, the range and the file name. Nothing starts yet. Read the statement, then press Render. Render again is offered only for renders you started in this browser, because only this browser remembers their full settings.
- What a render left out or warned about is under its row, in plain words.

Before your first render, the queue says so and offers **Choose a preset**, which takes you to the preset list.

## Share it for review

The **Share for review** section, beside the film, makes a link that shows one finished film to someone without an account. Press **Create a review link**, set its options and copy the link when it appears: it is shown only once. The links you made are listed under it with their views and notes, and each can be turned off. See [Share a film for review](share-for-review.md).

## On a phone

Tap **Export** in the top bar. The sheet opens full height: pick a format (Vertical for phones comes first), check the price line, and press **Render**, which stays at the bottom of the sheet so you never scroll for it. The range, the file name, how captions are delivered and the loudness choices are under **More options**.

While a film renders you can leave the page. When you come back, the list is refreshed. If a film finished while the sheet was closed, **Export** shows a green **Ready** mark and you get a message on screen. The finished film plays right there. **Download** saves it under its own name, and **Share film** hands the file to another app when your phone allows it (otherwise **Share link** shares the project link, and if your phone cannot share at all the button is not shown). We do not send an email when a film is done.

## Downloading from other pages

On your project's Renders tab and on a workflow step's result, **Download** saves the video as a file instead of opening it in the browser.
