# See what is running in the background

Some jobs take a while: making your film, studying your media, blurring an object, asking for edit ideas. They run in the background, so you can keep working. ReelBolt shows them in one place.

## See what is running

The activity button sits in the header of every page. It shows how many jobs are running, a spinner while one is, and a dot when something failed or is waiting for you. Click it to open the list.

The list has three parts:

- **Needs your OK.** Jobs that finished, but need a decision from you (see below).
- **Running now.** Jobs that are running, or waiting for a free slot.
- **Recently finished.** What finished, failed or was cancelled in the last day. Select **Clear finished** to tidy the list.

Each row says what the job is, which project it belongs to, and how far along it is when ReelBolt knows. A job that costs credits shows about how many credits are held while it runs, and how many were used when it ends. Select **Open** on a row to go to the place where the job's work lives.

Everyone in your workspace who can view projects sees the jobs of the workspace. Jobs you started yourself are the ones that notify you.

## See the job on the clip

A job that works on a clip shows on that clip in the timeline. A strip runs along the bottom of the clip, filling as the job goes, or sliding when it cannot say how far along it is. The clip is slightly dimmed and says **Processing**. This covers blurring an object, cleaning up sound, AI edit ideas, following a subject and preparing a stabilised preview.

- **Ready to review** shows on the clip when the job finished and needs your OK. Click it to open the result.
- **Did not finish** shows on the clip when the job failed. Point at it to read why.

See [Pages and panels](./pages-and-panels.md#a-clip-that-is-being-processed) for what the editor holds while a job runs.

## Cancel a job

Select **Cancel** on a row, then **Yes, stop it**. Select **Keep going** to change your mind. A job you cannot stop does not show the button.

## When a job fails

The row says what went wrong in plain words. If your credits were returned, it says so ("Your credits were given back."). If not, it tells you that the credits used up to the failure were kept.

## Finished, needs your OK

You can keep editing while a job such as a blur, a picture edit or a set of AI edit ideas runs. If your edits changed the clip it was made for, ReelBolt does not guess and does not throw the result away. The job moves to **Needs your OK** with a short sentence about what changed:

- Select **Apply** to use the result on what is left of the clip. It is one step, so **Undo** takes it back in one go.
- Select **Discard** to drop it.

If you delete a picture layer while a picture job is working on it, the result is kept as a hidden layer. Show it to keep it, or discard it.

If you are about to cut, move, split or change the speed of a clip a job is still working on, the editor asks first. It also asks before you change the clip's footage, or something else the job depends on, such as the crop or position while a subject is being followed. You can carry on (the result follows your edit), stop the job, or go back.

## Get told when a job finishes

When a job you started finishes, fails or needs your OK while you are on another page, a short message appears with a link to **Open** it. You do not get one if you are already on the job's page.

To turn these messages off on this device, open the activity list and switch off **Tell me when a background job finishes**. The setting is per device and applies to successes and failures.

If you also allowed browser notifications for the assistant, you get a notification outside the tab when the page is hidden.

## The AI tools button in the editor

The editor has one **AI tools** button that lists every AI tool in one place: for pictures, sound, editing and checking. Each tool shows whether it is free or uses credits, and a short line on what it does. A tool that is running shows a mark on its row. If a tool needs a clip, or an in and out range, and you have not chosen one yet, the list tells you to select a clip first.

Choose a tool to open it. See [Blur or mask an object](./blur-or-mask-an-object.md) and [Pages and panels](./pages-and-panels.md) for the tools themselves.
