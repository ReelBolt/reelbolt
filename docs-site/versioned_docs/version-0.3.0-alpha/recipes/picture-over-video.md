# Show a picture or video over the film

Use this when the finished film should show a picture or a video that is not part of the footage: a
testimonial screenshot, a review or message, a price table, a logo, a product clip or a second
angle. You put the pictures in a slot; the editing agent picks which one to show, at which calm
moment, and whether it takes the whole screen or a corner.

## Choose a full-screen picture or a corner

| What you want to show | Choose | Why |
|---|---|---|
| A screenshot, review, message, price table or document the viewer must read | **Full screen** | The picture fills the frame long enough to be read. The film's own picture is covered while it is up. |
| A logo, product clip, second angle, or a "look at this" reference | **In a corner** | The speaker and the film stay visible; the inset sits at an edge, away from the centre. |

The agent makes this choice per picture. A full-screen picture is fitted whole, so a tall
screenshot keeps its shape with bars on the sides rather than being cropped. A corner picture
matches its own shape and sits at one of nine positions (corners, edges or centre) at a small,
medium or large size.

## Put your pictures in the slot

Start the **Highlight edit from footage** workflow. Its start form has **Your clips** for the
footage and **A picture or video to show** for the pictures and videos that go over it.

- Add up to four pictures or videos. Each must be an image or a video that is already in this
  project.
- **Leave the slot empty** and the agent may choose any usable picture or video in the project.
- **Add files** and only those files are offered, in the order you added them. The agent chooses
  among them; it cannot reach outside the set.
- You do not pick the exact second in this slot. The agent chooses from the calm moments the edit
  kept. To steer it, say the moment in **Your brief**: "show the review when she says the app saved
  her an hour".

You can also ask the assistant to pin a picture exactly: "show `testimonial.png` full screen from
0:12 to 0:18". The assistant shows you what it is about to save before it writes anything, and it
does not start the run.

## Choose what happens to the film's sound

Under a full-screen picture, the film's own sound normally continues. Choose **Under a full-screen
picture** in the start form:

- **Let the agent choose** (default) — the agent keeps or mutes the sound per picture.
- **Keep the sound** — the speaker keeps talking under the picture.
- **Mute the sound** — the film's sound fades out for the picture and comes back after it. A sound
  the picture itself brings still plays.

A picture in a corner never changes the film's sound. A video shown in the film can bring its own
sound, which the agent mixes at a level it chooses — quiet, balanced or prominent, never boosted
above the clip's own level.

## What the run card shows

When the run finishes, the run card has a **Pictures over the film** section. **What was shown**
lists one row for each picture that appears:

- the file's name;
- **Full screen** or **Corner (bottom right)**;
- the time range, such as `0:12–0:18`;
- the opacity when it is below 100%;
- a light or dark border, when there is one;
- **sound kept** or **sound muted** for a full-screen picture, or **sound at Quiet / Balanced /
  Prominent** for a video with its own sound.

Only pictures that were actually shown appear here. You see names and plain words, not file ids or
coordinates.

## What "Not shown" means

Below the list, **Not shown** names the pictures that were offered but did not appear, with a plain
reason:

- **the moment was cut out** — the part of the film where the picture would have gone is not in the
  edit;
- **that picture could not be read** — the file is damaged, not a picture or video, or cannot be
  opened;
- **not chosen for this edit** — the agent chose a different picture;
- **there was room for only a few pictures** — the picture was beyond the number the film can hold;
- **it was set to be invisible** — its opacity is zero;
- **it was chosen twice** — the same picture and moment were planned twice and only one was used;
- **that picture is too large** — the file is over the size or length limit for a picture over the film.

**Not shown** is information, not an error: the run still succeeded and produced a film. If a
picture you expected is missing, name it in **Your brief** or ask the assistant to pin it.

## What is not done

- **No text reading.** A screenshot is shown as a picture. ReelBolt does not read, extract, search
  or check the words inside it as text.
- **The picture does not follow a moving screen.** If a phone or laptop screen should fill with
  your picture and move with it, use [Put an animation on a green screen](tracked-screen-insert.md)
  instead.
- **No free placement by dragging.** You choose full screen or a corner; the agent places the
  corner at an edge and size it picks. The assistant and the Advanced editor can pin the form,
  edge, size and moment, but not an arbitrary pixel position.
- **No separate transition for a picture.** The film's normal fade applies around it.
- **No per-picture ducking.** A video with its own sound sits at one fixed level under the film.

## Pitfalls

- **Nothing is shown.** The slot is empty and the agent chose none, or the project has no usable
  picture. Name the picture in **Your brief**, or put it in the slot and ask explicitly.
- **The picture appears at the wrong moment.** The agent can only use the calm moments the edit
  kept. Say the moment in **Your brief**, or ask the assistant to pin an exact time range.
- **A full-screen picture covers the speaker for too long.** Ask for a corner, or pick a shorter
  moment. A full-screen picture is meant for something the viewer must read.
- **The picture looks cropped or has bars.** A full-screen picture is fitted whole and adds bars
  when its shape differs from the film; a corner picture keeps its own shape. Check the original
  file if the result surprises you.
