# Events lane

The **Events lane** shows the moments that matter in your video: something appears, something starts or stops moving, a hit, text coming in, the camera starting to move, a beat, someone starting to speak. Each moment is a small mark on the timeline. Tap or click one and the playhead goes to the exact frame, and one menu lets you act on it.

It is a way of looking at your video. Nothing in it is saved in your film, and showing or hiding it changes nothing you export.

## Show the lane {#show-the-lane}

Open **Events** in the timeline toolbar and choose **Show events lane**, or press **E**. The lane appears under the ruler and the marker lane, and the choice is remembered for this timeline on this device.

The first time, a video has no events yet. Choose **Find events in this video** in the same menu. The menu tells you the price before anything runs: finding events is free. It takes a moment, and the marks appear when it is done.

## Read the marks {#read-the-marks}

- **Colour and shape** tell the kind of moment. The shape means the same thing in any colour, so you never have to tell colours apart: a diamond for something appearing (empty when it disappears), a triangle for movement or the camera starting, a square for a stop, text or speech (empty when it ends), a dot for a beat, a cross for a transition and a bar for a hit or a change.
- **Height** is how strong the moment is. A tall mark is a clear one.
- Zoom in and each mark gets its name. Hover over a mark, or move to it with the keyboard, to see its kind and time.

Only marks on the video clips of your timeline are shown, at the place where that clip plays them. Trim or move a clip and its marks follow.

## Snap to events {#snap-to-events}

With the lane shown, the playhead and the edges of clips snap to the marks as they do to other clips' edges. The magnet at the left of the lane shows whether this is on, and **Snap to events** in the **Events** menu (or **Shift+N**) turns it off. It is on by default, and the timeline's own snapping switch still has the last word.

## Act on an event {#act-on-an-event}

Right-click a mark, tap it on a tablet, or choose it with the keyboard (**Left** and **Right**, then **Enter**). **Shift+E** opens the menu for the event nearest the playhead. The menu lists what you can do right there:

- **Add sound here** moves the playhead to the event and opens the Media page, so you can pick a sound and place it exactly on the moment.
- **Add title here** adds a title that starts on the event.
- **Zoom here** punches in on the event for about a second and eases back, centred on where it happened when the editor knows where that is.
- **Pan here** drifts the picture over a second, towards the event when it knows where that is.
- **Shake** adds a short camera shake that dies down. The same event always gives the same shake.
- **Flash** adds a few white frames on the event.
- **Speed ramp** slows the half second after the event to half speed.
- **Cut here** splits the clip, and its linked sound, on the event's frame.

Every choice is one step in the undo history, so one **Undo** takes it back. The choices are exact because they use the event's own frame, not an estimate. An action that does not make sense for a mark, for example **Zoom here** on a clip that is locked, is left out of the menu rather than greyed out.

## On a phone {#on-a-phone}

The phone has no lane and no new gesture. Open **More**, then **Events at the playhead**. The sheet names the event nearest the playhead, **Previous event** and **Next event** move between events, and each action is a large button. It also has **Find events in this video** when a video has none yet.

## Keyboard {#keyboard}

| Key | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| **E** | Show or hide the lane |
| **Shift+N** | Snap to events on or off |
| **Shift+E** | Act on the event nearest the playhead |
| **Left** / **Right** | Move between events when the lane has focus |
| **Enter** | Open the actions for the event |

You can change these in the keyboard shortcuts.
